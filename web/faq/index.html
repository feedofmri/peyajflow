PeyajFlow FAQ

Helping onion farmers make better post-harvest decisions. 44 questions, with sources.

Core idea: PeyajFlow is an onion-focused decision-support platform that helps farmers compare whether to sell now, look for a better market or buyer, or store and sell later by considering price, storage, logistics, costs, risks, cash urgency, and buyer opportunities.

ACore Concept & Problem 7

01What is PeyajFlow?

PeyajFlow is a decision-support platform designed specifically for onion farmers. Instead of only showing current market prices, it helps a farmer answer a more practical question: “What should I do with my onions right now?” It compares options such as selling immediately, searching for a better market or buyer, or storing the onions and selling later. The recommendation considers expected price, transportation cost, storage cost, spoilage or loss risk, urgency for cash, and available buyer opportunities.

02Why did you choose onions?

We chose onions because onion prices in Bangladesh can be highly volatile, farmers frequently face losses during harvest season, storage is a major challenge, and onions are nationally important. Onion farming also fits the PeyajFlow decision model well because the farmer often has three realistic options after harvest: sell now, search for another market, or store and sell later.

03How is PeyajFlow different from existing agricultural market-price apps?

Existing services may show prices, connect buyers and sellers, or support logistics. PeyajFlow goes one step further by combining market price, buyer opportunities, logistics cost, storage feasibility, expected loss, and future selling potential into a single onion-specific decision workflow. Its goal is not just to provide information but to turn that information into an actionable recommendation.

04Does PeyajFlow predict future onion prices?

PeyajFlow can use historical and current market information to estimate possible future selling conditions, but it should not be presented as a system that can predict prices perfectly. The goal is better-informed decision-making under uncertainty, not guaranteed forecasting.

05What happens if the price prediction is wrong?

PeyajFlow should show expected outcomes together with risk and uncertainty rather than present a future price as guaranteed. For example, selling now may have lower uncertainty, while storing for one month may offer a higher expected return but also higher storage and market risk. The farmer can then understand the trade-off before deciding.

06Why wouldn’t the farmer always choose to store and sell later?

Because storage creates cost and risk. The farmer must consider storage cost, spoilage, weight loss, quality deterioration, future price uncertainty, transport cost, and the need for immediate cash. A higher future selling price does not automatically mean a higher or safer profit.

07What if the farmer urgently needs money?

Cash urgency should be treated as an important decision factor. If the farmer needs payment immediately, PeyajFlow may recommend selling all or part of the harvest now rather than waiting for a potentially higher future price. A future version could also recommend a split strategy, such as selling one portion now and storing the rest.

BAccess, Data & Decision Engine 12

08Why do you need AI voice support?

Voice support can reduce the digital-literacy barrier. A farmer could describe the situation in Bangla, such as quantity, location, cash need, and storage availability, and the system could return a recommendation in an easy conversational form.

09Where will PeyajFlow get its market-price data?

A production version could combine government agricultural market data, wholesale-market information, buyer quotations, partner traders, historical records, farmer or field-agent reports, and verified transaction data generated through the platform. Data should be timestamped and verified because recommendation quality depends heavily on data quality.

10How will you prevent traders or users from submitting fake prices?

Possible safeguards include verified buyer accounts, transaction history, multiple-source price confirmation, outlier detection, reputation scoring, timestamped quotations, and field-agent verification. The system can also attach a confidence level to market information.

11Is PeyajFlow an e-commerce marketplace?

Not exactly. A marketplace can be one component, but the core product is the decision-support system. PeyajFlow first helps the farmer decide what action is best, and then buyer matching, logistics, or transaction services can help execute that decision.

12Is PeyajFlow replacing middlemen?

No. Traders, wholesalers, transporters, storage operators, and intermediaries often perform necessary functions in the agricultural supply chain. PeyajFlow aims to make the process more transparent and efficient, and reliable intermediaries can become verified buyers or service partners.

13What type of AI will PeyajFlow use?

Different parts of the system may use different approaches: time-series or machine-learning models for price estimation, risk models for spoilage and storage, optimization algorithms for comparing options, natural-language processing for Bangla voice interaction, and recommendation methods for matching farmers with buyers. AI is a tool within the decision system, not the product by itself.

14What information would a farmer need to provide?

Initially, only essential information should be needed: location, onion quantity, harvest date, approximate quality, desired payment timeline, storage availability, and transport availability. The platform should obtain market and buyer information automatically wherever possible.

15What if a farmer enters incorrect information?

Recommendation quality will depend partly on the quality of the inputs. PeyajFlow can reduce this risk using input validation, standardized units, suggested ranges, historical farmer records, agent verification, and eventually photo-based quality checks. The system should also indicate when confidence in a recommendation is low.

16How will you calculate storage loss?

Storage-loss estimates can depend on storage type, temperature, humidity, storage duration, onion variety, initial quality, ventilation, and historical loss rates. The expected physical and economic loss would then be included in the decision calculation.

17What if no storage facility is available near the farmer?

Then storage should not be treated as a realistic option. PeyajFlow should first check storage feasibility and compare only the choices that can actually be executed, such as the local market and alternative buyers.

18How will you handle transportation?

Transportation cost must be included in the recommendation. A higher price in another market may not be better once delivery, handling, distance, and time are considered. PeyajFlow should compare net returns, not only headline prices.

19Can PeyajFlow guarantee that a farmer will make more money?

No. Agricultural markets involve uncertainty from price changes, weather, spoilage, buyers, transport disruptions, and other factors. PeyajFlow can provide a better-informed and more transparent decision process, but it cannot responsibly guarantee profit.

CUsers, Business Model & Pilot 17

20Who are your target customers?

The initial users are onion farmers and buyers. The wider ecosystem can include cooperatives, traders, wholesalers, retailers, storage providers, transport companies, agricultural extension organizations, government agencies, NGOs, and development organizations.

21Who will pay for PeyajFlow?

A practical model should avoid putting a heavy burden on small farmers. Revenue could come from buyer subscriptions, transaction commissions, logistics or storage partnerships, premium analytics, institutional subscriptions, or government and NGO partnerships. Basic farmer decision support could remain free or subsidized.

22What is your initial business model?

A strong early model is B2B2F — Business-to-Business-to-Farmer. Instead of depending only on farmers to pay, PeyajFlow can create revenue from organizations that benefit from reliable supply, logistics coordination, or market intelligence.

23How will you initially launch the project?

The presentation proposes a deliberately small pilot: around 30–50 farmers, 5–10 buyers, and 1 producer cluster. The strategy is Build & Pilot → Validate → Expand over the following years.

24Why start with only 30–50 farmers?

Because the purpose of the first stage is validation, not rapid scale. A small pilot allows the team to test whether farmers understand the recommendations, the price data are reliable, buyers participate, storage advice is realistic, and farmer outcomes improve before expansion.

25How will you measure whether the pilot is successful?

Useful indicators include additional income per farmer, reduction in avoidable losses, recommendation adoption, price improvement compared with the farmer’s normal option, successful buyer connections, storage outcomes, repeat usage, farmer satisfaction, and buyer retention. A strong key metric would be the improvement in farmer net realization per maund compared with the normal selling option.

26What is PeyajFlow’s main innovation?

The innovation is the decision layer. Instead of only telling the farmer the market price, PeyajFlow combines market opportunities, storage, logistics, costs, risk, and farmer-specific constraints into an actionable recommendation.

27Do farmers really need an AI system for this?

AI is not the objective. The objective is solving a difficult decision problem. AI is useful when the system must evaluate many variables, estimate future conditions, personalize recommendations, or communicate naturally. Where a simple rule works better, the product should use a simple rule.

28How do you build farmer trust?

Trust should come from transparent results. During the pilot, the system should explain recommendations clearly, show the main calculation behind the advice, keep the final decision with the farmer, work through trusted local partners, track actual outcomes, and communicate both successful and unsuccessful cases.

29Why would buyers join PeyajFlow?

Buyers benefit from access to verified supply, quantity information, location, harvest timing, and more efficient sourcing. The platform can reduce search cost and improve procurement planning, so it creates value on both sides of the market.

30Could buyers use the system to push prices down?

That is a risk in any marketplace. PeyajFlow should reduce it by giving farmers multiple competing offers and independent market information. The platform should increase price transparency rather than allow one buyer to control the farmer’s information.

31How will you make money without becoming another middleman?

The difference is transparency. PeyajFlow can charge clearly disclosed service or transaction fees while showing the farmer the expected net income after logistics, storage, and fees. The farmer can then decide whether the service creates enough value.

32What happens if farmers simply ignore your recommendation?

That is acceptable. PeyajFlow is a decision-support system, not a decision-control system. Farmers retain the final decision. Recommendation acceptance and actual outcomes can also be used to improve the system over time.

33How scalable is PeyajFlow?

The core concept is scalable because the central product is software and decision intelligence. Expansion can move from more farmers to more districts, more buyers, stronger storage and logistics networks, and later to other commodities. Scale should follow successful validation.

34Can this solution work for crops other than onions?

Yes, potentially. The framework could later be adapted to crops such as potato, garlic, tomato, chili, and other perishables. However, each commodity has different shelf life, storage-loss behavior, logistics, and price dynamics, so PeyajFlow should first prove itself for onions.

35Why call it “PeyajFlow”?

“Peyaj” immediately connects the product with onions in Bangladesh, while “Flow” represents the movement of information, decisions, produce, buyers, storage, logistics, and money across the supply chain.

36What national impact could PeyajFlow have?

The presentation illustrates that if Bangladesh reduced onion losses by only 1 percentage point, from 25% to 24%, about 42,450 tonnes of onions could be retained, representing roughly Tk 254 crore in commodity value according to the presentation’s calculation. The example shows how small efficiency improvements can create meaningful national impact.

DTough Questions 8

37Isn’t the real problem lack of storage rather than lack of information?

Storage is a major constraint, but storage alone does not solve the problem. Even when storage exists, the farmer still needs to know whether the expected future price justifies storage cost, spoilage risk, transport, and uncertainty. PeyajFlow does not replace storage infrastructure; it helps determine when storage should actually be used.

38Farmers already know local prices. Why would they need PeyajFlow?

Knowing the nearby market price is only one part of the decision. Farmers may not know whether another buyer offers more, whether transport is worthwhile, whether storage is economically viable, or whether waiting is worth the risk. PeyajFlow combines these variables into one decision.

39Isn’t this just another agricultural marketplace?

No. A marketplace mainly answers, “Who can buy my onions?” PeyajFlow first answers, “What should I do with my onions?” Buyer matching is only one possible action after that decision.

40What is your biggest risk?

The biggest early-stage risks are data reliability and farmer trust. Even a strong model is not useful if market prices are outdated or farmers do not trust the recommendation. That is why the pilot should focus on a small producer cluster, verified buyers, reliable local data, and comparison between recommended and actual outcomes.

41What is your biggest competitive advantage?

The main advantage is the decision layer: PeyajFlow integrates market, logistics, storage, risk, and farmer-specific constraints into one recommendation designed specifically for onion post-harvest decisions.

42What prevents another company from copying PeyajFlow?

The idea can be copied, but defensibility can grow from local market datasets, verified buyer networks, farmer transaction histories, storage-loss data, decision-model performance, distribution partnerships, and farmer trust.

43What happens during an extreme price shock?

PeyajFlow should update recommendations as new information arrives. During abnormal market conditions, uncertainty should be communicated more strongly, and the system should issue higher-risk warnings instead of pretending to know the future precisely.

44What stage is PeyajFlow currently at?

PeyajFlow is currently presented as a solution concept and pilot framework. The next step is to build the minimum viable product, onboard a small producer cluster and verified buyers, collect real transaction data, and evaluate whether the recommendations improve farmer outcomes.
